BACKGROUND BRIEFING — Horn of Africa and Ethiopia (analyst-curated, as of November 2026). Case documents take precedence over this text. Positions are those publicly reported at the dates given; do not invent quotes, numbers or events beyond this briefing and the case passages.

Ethiopia's position. Ethiopia is Africa's second most populous country and has been landlocked since Eritrea's independence (de facto 1991, formal 1993). Most of its trade passes through Djibouti's port. Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed has presented reliable sea access as an economic and strategic need since October 2023, while saying he prefers dialogue and mutual benefit and ruled out war with Eritrea (March 2025). Access for trade is not the same as territory, a military base or recognition of Somaliland, and officials, neighbours and analysts argue about which of these Ethiopia is really seeking.

Timeline of sea access. 1 Jan 2024: Ethiopia and Somaliland sign a memorandum on sea access; Somalia rejects it as a violation of its sovereignty (2 Jan). Feb 2024: Somalia and Türkiye sign a defence and economic cooperation agreement (Türkiye to help build and train Somalia's navy). Aug 2024: Egypt and Somalia sign a defence pact; Djibouti offers Ethiopia access through Tadjoura. 10 Oct 2024: Egypt, Eritrea and Somalia leaders meet in Asmara and pledge security cooperation and support for Somalia's territorial integrity. 11 Dec 2024: the Ankara Declaration, mediated by Türkiye, sets a commercial-access pathway for Ethiopia under Somali sovereignty. 26 Dec 2025: Israel becomes the first state to recognise Somaliland; Somalia, Egypt, Türkiye, Djibouti, the African Union and Saudi Arabia reject or criticise the move. Somaliland's status remains disputed; the African Union treats it as part of Somalia.

Tigray and the north. The war between the federal government and the TPLF ran from November 2020 to the Pretoria agreement of 2 November 2022 under African Union mediation; Eritrean forces fought alongside federal forces. Estimates of the dead range widely (about 80,000 to 600,000) and the true toll is unknown. A joint UN-Ethiopian Human Rights Commission report (November 2021) found violations by all sides. The Tigray interim administration changed leadership in 2025; the electoral board deregistered the TPLF in May 2025, which the TPLF rejects; relations between Addis Ababa and Asmara deteriorated, with reported fear of confrontation in early 2026. Ethiopia also faces armed conflict in Amhara and Oromia.

The Nile. Ethiopia inaugurated the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD) on 9 Sept 2025. Egypt calls it a unilateral step that threatens its water security and protested to the UN Security Council; Sudan is also downstream. Ethiopia says it will not significantly harm downstream states and offers shared benefits.

External actors. Egypt: GERD dispute, defence pact with Somalia, closeness to Eritrea. Türkiye: defence partner of Somalia, supplier of drones to Ethiopia, mediator of the Ankara Declaration. Eritrea: signed peace with Ethiopia in 2018, fears Ethiopian designs on its coast, part of the 2024 Asmara grouping. Djibouti: Ethiopia's main trade route, which sea-access diversification could affect. Saudi Arabia and the UAE: hosted and facilitated the 2018 Ethiopia–Eritrea peace and are Red Sea investors and security actors. Israel: long ties with Ethiopia and recognition of Somaliland. Somalia: insists on its territorial integrity but agreed to the Ankara process.
